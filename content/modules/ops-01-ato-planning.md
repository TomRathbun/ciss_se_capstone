## What is an ATO?

An **Air Tasking Order** is the plan that assigns air missions, resources, and timing for a period of operations. It turns commander intent into flyable tasks: who goes where, when, with what, talking how.

Think of it as the **published plan of record** for air activity in a window of time.

## Planning vs execution

| Planning | Execution |
|----------|-----------|
| Build missions before wheels up | Launch, monitor, adjust, recover |
| Match objectives to assets | React to weather, aborts, threats |
| Deconflict timing & airspace | Real-time C2 and reports |
| Loadout, fuel, tanker, comms plan | Divert, re-role, cancel, assess |

This module = **planning**. Next ops module = **execution**.

## Mission types (conceptual)

Interns should know these categories exist (local names may vary):

| Type | Intent (plain language) |
|------|-------------------------|
| Training / continuation | Build and keep aircrew proficiency |
| Air defense / CAP | Protect airspace or assets |
| Strike / interdiction | Engage designated targets (real-world or exercise) |
| Support / ISR / tanker | Enable others (sensors, fuel, C2) |
| Transport / lift | Move people or cargo |

You do **not** need classified tactics — you need the *planning vocabulary*.

## Planning factors checklist

When building a mission, planners consider at least:

1. **Objective** — what success looks like  
2. **Assets** — aircraft type and availability  
3. **Loadout** — weapons / stores concept matched to objective  
4. **Fuel & tankers** — range, loiter, refueling options  
5. **Timing** — takeoff, time-on-target / on-station, recovery  
6. **Route / airspace** — deconfliction with other missions  
7. **Threat / environment** — weather, known constraints (unclass level)  
8. **Comms** — voice nets; **datalinks** for digital SA  
9. **C2** — who owns the mission in execution  
10. **Contingencies** — abort, divert, weather backup

## Voice vs datalink (planning view)

| Path | Role in planning | Limitations (why you still plan the other path) |
|------|------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| **Voice** | Control, brevity, human coordination; **backup** when digital fails | Congestion, human latency, harder shared SA across many players |
| **Datalink** | Shared tracks / tasking efficiency when available | Needs working terminals, nets, and procedures; can degrade or drop |

Plan for **both**: digital preferred when up; voice always thought through.

### Sample mini-mission card — Air Defense CAP (training)

| Field | Example (fictitious) |
|-------|----------------------|
| Mission ID | CAP-01-2026-A |
| Mission type | Air defense / CAP |
| Objective | Provide continuous air cover over the North Sector training range for a 2-hour window. |
| Primary package | 2× F-16 (air-to-air concept) |
| Secondary / spare | 1× F-35 (flex) |
| Weapons / stores concept | Public A/A missile families only — no classified loadout |
| Tanker / fuel plan | No tanker; plan endurance within published public range class |
| Takeoff / on-station | Takeoff 0830Z; on-station 0900–1100Z |
| Comms | VCS Net-A primary, Net-B backup |
| Datalink | Yes — Link 16 concept for shared tracks when available |
| C2 agency | AOC / training C2 cell |
| Risks / contingencies | Low visibility → delay or voice-only procedures; abort → spare |
| Success criteria | Planned on-station coverage met; no unresolved unidentified tracks left unreported to C2 |

**Depth bar:** one screen of clear fields beats three pages of invented tactics.

| Dimension | Strong | Weak |
|-----------|--------|------|

| Communication | Peer can brief from the card in 2 minutes | Jargon without meaning; classified cosplay |

## Self-check (5 min)

1. What is an **ATO** in one sentence?  
2. Name three differences between **planning** and **execution**.  
3. List five items from the **planning factors** checklist.  
4. Why plan **both** voice and datalink?  
5. On a mission card, what belongs under **success criteria** (good vs weak example)?  

### Answers

1. The published plan that assigns air missions, resources, and timing for a period of operations.  
2. Examples: build vs fly; deconflict on paper vs real-time C2; loadout planning vs abort/re-role; tanker plan vs tanker slip response.  
3. Any five of: objective, assets, loadout, fuel/tankers, timing, route/airspace, threat/environment, comms, C2, contingencies.  
4. Datalink can fail (bandwidth, terminals, nets); voice is slower/shared but is the critical backup path.  
5. **Good:** observable (e.g. on-station minutes, coverage, C2 contact). **Weak:** “mission success” with no measurable signal.
